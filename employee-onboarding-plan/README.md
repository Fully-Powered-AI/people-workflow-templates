## The outcome

A new colleague arrives on Monday and the plan is already there: the first week day by day, and a clear picture of productive at day 30, 60 and 90, written as work the colleague will be doing.

Most companies have one manager who plans a start like this. This workflow template gives every hiring manager that standard, built from their own role and training material. The people lead sets it once and every start gets it. That is 10X what a small people team can do alone.

The manager adapts the plan and judges progress. The new colleague shapes it in week one.

## Who starts the work, and when

The hiring manager starts it once the start date is confirmed, ideally two weeks before day one. The people lead owns the company-wide parts: required training, first-day arrangements and the check-in rhythm. Manager and new colleague read it together on day one, and the manager updates it after each check-in.

## What they supply

- **The role’s agreed responsibilities.** The role brief agreed before recruiting, or a current job description.
- **The team’s training material.** Procedures, system guides and recorded walkthroughs, each with its date.
- **The start date and working pattern.** Hours, site and any remote days.
- **The people to meet.** By role, including the buddy.
- **The company’s onboarding standard.** Required training and first-day arrangements, set by the people lead.

## What comes out

A draft plan in four parts: what to confirm before day one, the first week, milestones at day 30, 60 and 90, and the check-ins. Every gap goes to the manager by name, such as a responsibility with no training material or a guide past the team’s age limit.

Access, equipment and invitations stay with the teams that own them. The workflow template never assesses the new colleague and never drafts an offer letter or other employment document.

## What has to change for your company

Set the standard first: the check-in rhythm, the age limit for training material, the training every start completes and how productive is written for your roles. Training material usually covers the routine case; capture the exceptions experienced colleagues handle with [SOP exception capture](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/sop-exception-capture/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=workflow-collections&utm_content=people-employee-onboarding-plan&from=gh-people-employee-onboarding-plan).

Map the handoffs to IT, facilities and the buddy so every confirmation has an owner. Prove it on one real start, then roll it out. FullyPowered works across ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and Gemini, at your pace, and your company keeps control of how the work gets done.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
